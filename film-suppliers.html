00

Stop — do not stock 5%

It is not legally installable in Florida

The meter reads glass and film together. 5% film on clear glass lands around 4%. The floors are 28% front sides, 15% behind the driver on a car, 6% behind the driver on an SUV or truck. 4% is under every one of them.

On a sedan it is plainly illegal. On an SUV rear it misses the 6% floor by about two points, and whether the ±3% tolerance in 316.2955 rescues it is a question you would be arguing with an officer, with your business name on the door-jamb label. Installing it is a second-degree misdemeanor on you, not a ticket on the driver.

Stock 15% instead. On clear glass it reads about 12% — legal on an SUV back half with room to spare, and to the customer's eye it is nearly as dark. When somebody asks for limo black on their Tahoe, 15% is the answer that does not end in court.

04

Reliability — what to be suspicious of

Uviron: cheapest, with two flags

The price is genuinely the lowest verified anywhere on this list and the review counts are large (1,646 on carbon). But: the customer testimonials on the page talk about a film called "TintX" while the product is sold as MAX CARBON — a sign of relabelled stock or a rebrand, not proof of either. And at least one review on the page itself calls the film "Walmart grade at best" and says not to buy.

So do not place a four-roll order with them sight unseen. Buy one roll, cut four windows on junkyard glass, and judge the shrink and the adhesive yourself. $140 is a cheap test; $720 of film you cannot work with is not.

Flexfilm: most transparent on the things that matter

The only supplier here that states, in writing, that no film in the line contains metal, gives a layer breakdown (2-ply, 1.5 mil), an IR figure (67% on carbon), a lifetime warranty bound to the original owner, and plotter compatibility. It costs about $19 more per car than Uviron carbon. For the ceramic jobs you charge $350–450 for, that difference is nothing and the warranty paper is worth having.

Tinters Supply: the local one

Miami Lakes, about 40 minutes away. Highest ceramic price of the three, but no shipping wait and a phone number to call — which is what matters when you have a car booked Saturday and you are out of 35%. Build the account before you need it, and ask on the phone: do they allow pickup, is there a trade price, and what is the minimum order.

What would make a film counterfeit or junk

  • "Ceramic" at a dyed-film price — under about $0.40/sq ft, be suspicious of the word.
  • No stated construction (ply count, mil thickness) and no IR or TSER figure.
  • Overseas direct shipping with a brand name you cannot find on that brand's own site.
  • No warranty, or a warranty with no registration process.
  • The complaints to search for before buying any roll: bubbling after a few months, purple fading, adhesive that will not release, film that creases rather than shrinks.
