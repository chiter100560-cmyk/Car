## 2. Coverage mechanics, plainly

**(a) GL "care, custody or control" (CCC) exclusion.** Liability forms exclude "'Property damage' to … d. Property in the 'insured's' care, custody or control" (ISO Garage Coverage Form CA 00 05 03 10, Section II exclusion 6) https://ogs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2021/09/ca-00-05-03-10.pdf . Practical meaning: "third-party claims caused by the customer's vehicle are covered, the vehicle itself is not," and garagekeepers exists to "buy back the coverage lost in the care, custody or control exclusion" https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2014/02/10/319390.htm . Simply Business says the same for detailers: GL "generally excludes damage to the cars you are working on" https://www.simplybusiness.com/business-insurance/auto-detailing-insurance/ . The garagekeepers insuring agreement (verbatim): "We will pay all sums the 'insured' legally must pay as damages for 'loss' to a 'customer's auto' … left in the 'insured's' care while the 'insured' is attending, servicing, repairing, parking or storing it in your 'garage operations' under: a. Comprehensive Coverage — From any cause except … collision … or overturn; b. Specified Causes Of Loss Coverage — Fire, lightning or explosion; Theft; or Mischief or vandalism; c. Collision Coverage" https://ogs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2021/09/ca-00-05-03-10.pdf . **Mobile trap:** the ISO form defines "garage operations" as "the ownership, maintenance or use of locations for garage business … [and] all operations necessary or incidental to a garage business" (same PDF), and the standard garagekeepers endorsement "applies only at locations on the endorsement schedule that have a limit of insurance and premium charge" https://www.rnc-pro.com/rnc-pro/pfm/200/220_0604.HTM — so a driveway-only business must get the policy written/endorsed for off-premises work (Pro Insurance Group: "specifically endorsed for mobile operations") https://www.proinsgrp.com/blog/what-is-garage-keepers-insurance . Ask every quoter, in writing: "Does garagekeepers apply at customers' residences and other off-premises locations?"

**(b) Legal liability vs direct primary vs direct excess.**
- Legal liability: "pays only if the shop is found legally responsible" https://www.lawrapandtintschool.com/post/business-insurance-for-window-tint-wrap-detail-shops-the-complete-2026-coverage-guide ; the standard endorsement provides "legal liability only unless a direct coverage option is selected" https://www.rnc-pro.com/rnc-pro/pfm/200/220_0604.HTM .
- Direct primary: "protects a customer's vehicle if it's damaged or stolen, no matter who is at fault … vandalism, extreme weather, and other events" https://www.insureon.com/auto-services-business-insurance/garage-keepers-insurance ; "your policy can respond before other available coverage" https://intrepiddirect.com/blog/garagekeepers-direct-primary-direct-excess-legal-liability .
- Direct excess: "a cheaper alternative to direct primary … covering losses exceeding the customer's personal auto insurance" https://www.insureon.com/auto-services-business-insurance/garage-keepers-insurance .
- Cost deltas: direct primary "25-50% more", direct excess "10-25% more" than legal liability https://www.proinsgrp.com/blog/what-is-garage-keepers-insurance .
- Which one for Ziyad: the tint/wrap trade guide is blunt — "Direct primary form — pays regardless of fault, like the customer's own collision policy. This is what you want" https://www.lawrapandtintschool.com/post/business-insurance-for-window-tint-wrap-detail-shops-the-complete-2026-coverage-guide ; Wexford: choose direct primary if "Working with high-value or luxury vehicles … Prioritize customer confidence" https://www.wexfordins.com/post/garagekeepers-direct-primary-vs-legal-liability ; Bellken recommends direct primary in coastal Florida because it "eliminates disputes about fault" https://www.bellkenins.com/commercial-insurance/garage-keeper-liability-insurance . My read for a driveway operation touching $30k–$100k cars: most realistic losses are employee mistakes (legal liability responds), but a hail/theft/flood loss during hurricane season or a fault fight with a $100k-car owner is exactly where legal-liability-only leaves you holding the bag. Buy direct primary if any quoter offers it at a tolerable price; otherwise legal liability with a serious per-vehicle limit — the limit matters more than the form (a $50k line will not fix a burned $100k car).

**(c) Typical limits and deductibles for a small detailer.** NEXT: $50k (Pro) / $100k (Pro Plus) per occurrence https://www.nextinsurance.com/business/car-wash-insurance/ . Progressive: per-vehicle deductible "typically $500" https://www.progressivecommercial.com/coverages/garage-keepers-liability/ . Landesblosch: policies carry per-vehicle, per-occurrence and aggregate limits; "Common deductibles … range from $250 to $1,000"; set limits by "the most expensive vehicle you will reasonably have … and the maximum vehicles" https://www.landesblosch.com/blog/garagekeepers-legal-liability-coverage-limits-and-more . Intrepid: per-auto deductibles "typically $1,000" with an event cap (e.g., "$5,000 maximum deductible per claim") https://intrepiddirect.com/blog/garagekeepers-direct-primary-direct-excess-legal-liability ; ISO wording: "the per 'loss' deductible … is the maximum deductible applicable for all 'loss' in any one event" https://ogs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2021/09/ca-00-05-03-10.pdf . Tint-trade guide: "$100,000 garagekeepers limit is the absolute floor in 2026" for a small tint shop; shops with "$180,000 Cybertruck or a $250,000 Porsche 911 GT3" need "$500,000 minimum"; deductibles "$500–$2,500"; South Florida high-end studios are "writing $1 million GKLL limits" https://www.lawrapandtintschool.com/post/business-insurance-for-window-tint-wrap-detail-shops-the-complete-2026-coverage-guide . Watch for sub-limits: some policies cap theft at "$25,000 per vehicle or $50,000 per event" https://www.proinsgrp.com/blog/what-is-garage-keepers-insurance . For Car Mommy: one car at a time, so per-occurrence ≈ per-vehicle; target $100k minimum, $150k–$250k if the customer mix is really up to $100k cars; $500–$1,000 deductible.

**(d) "Your work / faulty workmanship."** Garagekeepers exclusions, verbatim: "c. Defective Parts — Defective parts or materials. d. Faulty Work — Faulty 'work you performed'" https://ogs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2021/09/ca-00-05-03-10.pdf ; Insureon: excluded are "claims based on a complaint of faulty repair work or defective parts" https://www.insureon.com/auto-services-business-insurance/garage-keepers-insurance ; Reed Brothers: "will not cover if a customer says you did faulty or bad work" https://rbisomerset.com/blog/what-is-garagekeepers-insurance/ . GL has its own "your work" exclusion that "excludes the cost to repair or replace work the business itself performed poorly," though "GL may respond to injury or damage that failure causes to someone or something else" https://conexionins.com/blog/mobile-mechanic-insurance/ . Applied to tinting/detailing: bubbling, peeling, contaminated or mis-cut film = redo it yourself; no policy pays (NEXT sells a "Faulty Work" line that it says "can help you pay for: Redoing a job that wasn't up to par" — unusual; make them show you the form https://www.nextinsurance.com/business/car-wash-insurance/ ). Accidental physical damage during the job — dropped tool denting a hood, blade slip scratching glass, a rolled car — is "loss" to a customer's auto while "servicing" it and is the core garagekeepers claim (Conexion cites "a dropped tool that dents a hood" https://conexionins.com/blog/mobile-mechanic-insurance/ ). **Gray zone = damage caused by the technique itself** (defroster grid lines torn off during old-film removal, chemical etching, buffer burn-through): Simply Business says "workmanship" errors like "burning paint with a polisher" need "garage keepers primary coverage … or a 'care, custody, or control' endorsement" https://www.simplybusiness.com/business-insurance/auto-detailing-insurance/ , while a working detailer reports "insurance does not cover workmanship such as burning paint or dropping a buffer on the hood" https://autogeekonline.net/threads/garagekeepers-insurance.91687/ . Sources conflict because carriers apply the "faulty work" exclusion differently. Get it in writing from the underwriter: "Does the policy respond to accidental damage to glass, rear-defroster grids, trim, paint and electronics caused during installation or removal?" (Defroster mechanism, search snippet only, not fetched: razor removal can take "the rear window defroster with it" https://frostfighter.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Tech-109-Window-Tint-Defroster-Damage-and-Repair.pdf .)

**(e) Moving/test-driving the car; is commercial auto required?** Damage to the customer's car while Alex moves it in the driveway = garagekeepers **Collision** coverage ("the 'customer's auto's' collision with another object") https://ogs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2021/09/ca-00-05-03-10.pdf ; Insureon: garage keepers applies "when it's parked, being serviced, or during a test drive" https://www.insureon.com/blog/garage-keepers-vs-garage-liability-insurance . Injury/damage to third parties while driving it is a different policy: garagekeepers "will not pay to fix the car you pulled out in front of OR their hospital bills" https://rbisomerset.com/blog/what-is-garagekeepers-insurance/ , and the CGL auto exclusion removes BI/PD "arising out of the ownership, maintenance, use or entrustment to others of any … 'auto' … owned or operated by or rented or loaned to any insured"; the CG 22 68 exception for servicing customer vehicles applies "on the premises" https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/the-auto-exclusion-in-the-cgl-policy . A personal auto policy will not fill this: the PAP excludes vehicles "Used in the business of selling, repairing, servicing, storing, or parking vehicles," an exclusion that "applies to customer vehicles you may drive during business operations" but "does not exclude coverage for your own vehicles that are designated as 'your covered autos'" https://www.americaninsuranceid.com/faq/personal-insurance/auto-insurance/does-my-personal-auto-policy-exclude-business-use . Legally no commercial auto policy is "required" to detail cars in driveways; practically, if anyone ever drives a customer's car, you need garage liability or a commercial auto/HNOA line to cover third-party liability. Progressive: commercial auto is "a crucial insurance coverage for mobile auto detailing businesses because personal auto insurance often doesn't cover vehicles while being used for business purposes" https://www.progressivecommercial.com/business-insurance/professions/auto-detailing-insurance/ . Simplest rule for now: nobody drives customer cars beyond repositioning in the driveway, and only after HNOA/garage liability is confirmed in writing.

**(f) Hired & non-owned auto (HNOA) and the BMW.** HNOA covers third-party liability when employees use personal cars for business; "Physical damage to the hired or non-owned vehicle isn't covered"; "Personal auto policies usually exclude business use"; HNOA "is usually only available as an endorsement to a commercial auto, general liability, or BOP" https://www.progressivecommercial.com/business-resources/hired-and-non-owned-insurance/ . "The driver's personal auto policy defends the driver. It doesn't defend your company" https://www.menloinsurance.com/library/guides/hired-and-non-owned-auto-insurance . Infinity: "A personal auto policy may exclude or limit some business use" https://www.infinityauto.com/knowledge-center/small-business-tips/mobile-detailers-insurance-guide . For Ziyad: the BMW is his "covered auto," so driving it to jobs is generally still covered on his PAP under the auto-business exception above, but the policy's own business-use wording must be checked and the insurer told about business use; Alex driving his own car to jobs, or anyone driving a customer's car, is the HNOA/garage-liability exposure. Trade guide budget for HNOA: "$300–$700 per year" https://www.lawrapandtintschool.com/post/business-insurance-for-window-tint-wrap-detail-shops-the-complete-2026-coverage-guide . When a commercial van arrives, it needs a commercial auto policy (FL commercial auto averages "$230 per month ($2,763 per year)" https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/business/florida/cost/ ).

**(g) Inland marine / tools & equipment.** Standard property coverage only applies "at the location listed on the policy"; inland marine covers property "in transit over land or stored at an off-site location," averages "$48 per month" for Insureon customers, and is "added to your business owner's policy (BOP)" https://www.insureon.com/small-business-insurance/inland-marine . Simply Business prices tools "From $20.83/month" https://www.simplybusiness.com/business-insurance/auto-detailing-insurance/ . Schedule polishers, extractors, pressure washer, generator, plotter/laptop and film inventory in the BMW.

**(h) Alex and Rasul: employee vs 1099, subcontractor exclusions, Florida WC threshold.**
- Florida statute: "Employment" (private, non-construction) means "four or more employees"; construction is "one or more" — s. 440.02, 2025 text as fetched at subsection (20)(b) (older prints number it (17)(b); verify numbering) https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/440.02 . Florida DFS: "Employers with four (4) or more employees, including business owners who are corporate officers or Limited Liability Company (LLC) members, must have workers' compensation coverage," and "Non-construction industry Sole Proprietors or partners in a Partnership are not employees unless they want to be included … and file a form DWC-251" https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/employer/coverage-requirements . So as a sole proprietor Ziyad does not count; Alex + Rasul = 2 employees → WC not yet mandatory. If he forms an LLC and is a member he counts unless he files an exemption (LLC member must "attest to a minimum 10 percent ownership") https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/employer/exemptions/non-construction .
- 1099 reality check: under s. 440.02 the worker is an independent contractor only if at least four of six tests apply — separate business "with his or her own work facility, truck, equipment, materials"; a FEIN; pay "paid to a business rather than to an individual"; business bank account; free to work for others; paid "on a competitive-bid basis or completion of a task" — otherwise the presumption favors employee status https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/440.02 . Alex using Car Mommy's film, tools, customers and brand, paid personally, is an employee under Florida WC law regardless of what the paperwork says.
- Why it matters even under 4 employees: liability forms exclude "'Bodily injury' to: a. An 'employee' of the 'insured' arising out of and in the course of: (1) Employment by the 'insured'" (Employee Indemnification And Employer's Liability exclusion) https://ogs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2021/09/ca-00-05-03-10.pdf — so if Alex gets a chemical burn or falls, nothing pays unless you buy WC (detailing WC averages "$131 per month" nationally https://www.insureon.com/auto-services-business-insurance/car-detailing-wash/cost ; FL average "$103 per month" https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/business/florida/cost/ ). HOAs also expect vendors to carry WC https://blog.vendorsmart.com/the-hoa-guide-to-vendor-insurance-requirements/ .
- Subcontractor exclusions: "subcontractors operate independently and are typically not covered under the contractor's insurance policy" https://www.westfieldinsurance.com/about-us/articles/subcontractor-insurance-what-contractors-need-to-know-about-coverage-and-liability ; "your business insurance typically does not cover independent contractors you hire" https://www.illumend.ai/insurance-knowledge/does-my-business-insurance-cover-independent-contractors ; small-business GL forms often carry subcontractor limitation endorsements requiring subs' certificates, minimum limits and additional-insured status, with penalties up to a "hammer clause" nullifying coverage for losses from the sub's work https://www.amwins.com/resources-insights/article/contractor-s-general-liability-11-common-coverage-limitation-endorsements . Translation: if you call Alex a "1099 sub" on the application, the carrier may treat his work as uncovered unless he carries his own GL. Disclose him as an employee (and Rasul as a trainee employee); the application will ask "number of employees" and price on payroll/headcount https://www.infinityauto.com/knowledge-center/small-business-tips/mobile-detailers-insurance-guide . ## 4. Practical points

**Sole proprietor + DBA.** Yes, a sole proprietor can buy all of these. The named insured must be the person: "The named insured should be the owner's name and the business/operating name should be shown under 'dba'" — i.e., "Ziyad [Surname] dba Car Mommy" https://coterieinsurance.com/blog/who-should-be-the-named-insured-on-commercial-insurance-policies/ . Insurers are "unlikely to agree to add the name unless it is filed with the state" https://www.iamagazine.com/2023/01/27/scheduling-an-unfiled-dba-as-a-named-insured/ . Florida's Fictitious Name Act (s. 865.09) requires registering the DBA "prior to conducting business in Florida"; fee "$50.00"; you self-certify a one-time newspaper ad https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/start-business/efile/fl-fictitious-name-registration/ . If an LLC is formed later, the policy has to be re-issued or endorsed to the LLC as first named insured (same principle as above).

**Certificate of insurance (COI) and why HOAs ask.** A COI is "a document that shows proof of your business insurance and specific details about your policy" — policies, limits, dates, additional insureds; clients/landlords ask for it before "Allowing contract work" https://www.nextinsurance.com/certificate-of-insurance/ . HOAs require it because "If a vendor commits any wrongful act, community associations can face lawsuits or even become liable themselves"; typical ask is "general liability with a $1 million minimum limit," the HOA named as "additional insured" on GL and auto, plus WC https://blog.vendorsmart.com/the-hoa-guide-to-vendor-insurance-requirements/ . NEXT issues "unlimited certificates" instantly with additional insureds https://www.nextinsurance.com/business/car-wash-insurance/ ; keep a copy in the rig for property managers https://blog.detailking.com/insurance-for-auto-detailing-and-mobile-auto-detailing-businesses/ . No Parkland/Coral Springs-specific vendor rules were found; expect gate/management companies to ask for the standard $1M GL COI with additional-insured wording.

**Application questions that trip up mobile detailers.** Carriers ask for business name/structure/location, services, frequency and revenue, employee and vehicle counts, equipment value, "Travel radius and job locations," claims history, limits/deductibles https://www.infinityauto.com/knowledge-center/small-business-tips/mobile-detailers-insurance-guide ; "Vehicle usage" and "Services offered" drive price https://www.wexfordins.com/post/auto-detailing-business-insurance-complete-guide ; "Whether you have a mobile unit or a physical location" is a NEXT rating question https://www.nextinsurance.com/business/car-wash-insurance/ . Specific traps: (1) pressure washing is rated as its own class by some carriers (Insurance Canopy sells "Pressure Washing Insurance" separately https://www.insurancecanopy.com/cleaning-business-insurance/pressure-washing — search result, page not fetched); answer honestly if you use one. (2) Paint correction/polishing/chemicals: burned paint and wrong-chemical damage are treated as "workmanship" by Simply Business and pushed to garage keepers primary/CCC https://www.simplybusiness.com/business-insurance/auto-detailing-insurance/ — disclose these services. (3) "Do you drive customer vehicles?" — answering yes triggers driving-record checks and HNOA/garage liability https://blog.detailking.com/insurance-for-auto-detailing-and-mobile-auto-detailing-businesses/ . (4) Employees vs subs — see 2(h). (5) Be rated as detailing/"auto laundry," not auto repair https://blog.detailking.com/insurance-for-auto-detailing-and-mobile-auto-detailing-businesses/ . (6) Max vehicle value you service — this sets the garagekeepers limit https://www.landesblosch.com/blog/garagekeepers-legal-liability-coverage-limits-and-more .

**If a customer claims damage.** Prevention first: a "Full 360° exterior walkaround, all four wheels, dashboard with cluster powered on (warning lights visible), odometer, fuel level, interior condition, trunk, every visible pre-existing flaw," then "get them to initial it … a timestamped PDF from arrival, signed off by the customer, makes any later claim very hard to sustain" https://carphotochecklist.com/use-cases/shop-intake/ ; working detailers use a signed "Limitation of Liability & Agreement of Service" plus inspection form and photos/video — one caught a pre-cracked part only because "Went back to the pics and saw it was already cracked" https://autogeekonline.net/threads/paperwork-client-to-sign-off-before-after.65070/ ; template intake forms record "Pre-Existing Damage Notes" and e-signature and are built for "mobile detailers" https://www.jotform.com/form-templates/car-detailing-liability-waiver . For tint specifically, photograph the rear-defroster grid powered on before removal/installation. When a claim comes: the policy conditions require "prompt notice," and you must "Assume no obligation, make no payment or incur no expense without our consent, except at the 'insured's' own cost," forward any legal papers immediately, and cooperate https://ogs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2021/09/ca-00-05-03-10.pdf — so do not admit fault in writing or promise repairs before calling the carrier. Paying a small dent out of pocket is allowed and is sometimes cheaper because "Filing a claim under any insurance will often raise the monthly cost" https://blog.detailking.com/why-insurance-is-an-absolute-must-for-your-auto-detailing-business/ , but still notify the carrier if the customer is threatening a lawsuit.
