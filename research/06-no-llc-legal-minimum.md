## 1. Fictitious name (DBA) — Fla. Stat. 865.09

- **Required before doing business as "Car Mommy."** 865.09(3)(a): "A person may not engage in business under a fictitious name unless the person first registers the name with the division." Sunbiz: register if the name is "Different from your personal name, if doing business as a sole proprietor." https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/865.09 ; https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/start-business/efile/fl-fictitious-name-registration/
- **Fee: $50** (registration and renewal each $50; certified copy $30, certificate of status $10). https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/forms/fees/ and 865.09 fee text "For registration of a fictitious name, $50."
- **Newspaper ad first, then register — but it's an attestation only.** 865.09(3)(a) requires "Certification by at least one registrant that the intention to register such fictitious name has been advertised at least once in a newspaper as defined in chapter 50 in the county in which the principal place of business of the registrant is or will be located." Sunbiz: "proof of advertisement is not required. You, the applicant, certify the name has been advertised when you sign the application." The paper form's certification adds that false information "constitutes a third degree felony as provided for in s.817.155, F.S." — so do actually run the ad. Form: http://form.sunbiz.org/pdf/cr4e001.pdf
- **What "newspaper" qualifies:** Ch. 50.011 — printed/published at least weekly, ≥25% English, available to the public for notices, audience ≥10% of county households or periodical-class mail eligibility, etc. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/50.011 (the website-publication alternative in 50.0311 is for governmental notices, not this).
- **Cost in Broward:** Broward Daily Business Review publishes the fictitious-name notice one time for **$48.00**, prepaid, and sends proof of publication. https://images.law.com/media/dailybusinessreview/DBRPN/BC/Fictitous_Name_Broward/dbr_notice_under_fict_name_broward.pdf
- **Penalties for not registering:** 865.09(9)(a) — "neither the business nor the person… may maintain any action, suit, or proceeding in any court of this state… until this section is complied with" (you can be sued, you can't sue/enforce until you register — it's curable). 865.09(9)(c) — "a noncriminal violation as defined in s. 775.08, punishable as provided in s. 775.083." **Not a misdemeanor** (that belief is outdated); max fine $500 under 775.083(1)(e). https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/775.083
- **Term:** valid through "December 31 of the 5th calendar year" after registration; renew Jan 1–Dec 31 of the expiration year, $50 (865.09(5)–(6)).
- **No name rights:** 865.09(8): registration "is for public notice only, and does not give rise to a presumption of the registrant's rights to own or use the name." Sunbiz: it does not "Prohibit someone else from using or registering the same fictitious name."
- **Transfer to a future LLC:** 865.09(4) — on transfer of the business the old registrant cancels and the new person "may reregister the name… at the same time as the cancellation is filed." Form CR4E001 instructions: "TO CHANGE OWNERSHIP OF A REGISTRATION: Complete section 4 to cancel the original registration. Complete sections 1 through 3 to re-register… listing the new owner(s)." Fee: $50 non-refundable processing fee on that form.
- **Bank / insurer without an LLC:** SBA: what you need is an "Employer Identification Number (EIN)" or SSN for a sole proprietorship, formation documents, business license; "getting a DBA… combined with a federal tax ID number (EIN) allows you to open a business bank account." https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/open-business-bank-account. Progressive: "Sole proprietor business insurance is designed for companies that are owned and operated by one person" — GL, commercial auto, WC available without an LLC. https://www.progressivecommercial.com/business-insurance/sole-proprietor-insurance/ ; Next: "a sole proprietorship does not have any legal separation between the owner and their business… they're personally responsible." https://www.nextinsurance.com/blog/sole-proprietor-insurance/ (Bank-specific DBA document checklists and an insurer page stating the "Name DBA Car Mommy" format were not retrievable — see blocked list. Standard practice is that the named insured/account holder is Ziyad individually, with the DBA shown; treat that as unverified.)

## 2. Tax registrations

- **Florida sales & use tax (DR-1) as a sole proprietor, no LLC needed.** DR-1N: "If you are a sole proprietor (individual business owner)… you will need your social security number (SSN) to register" (entities use an FEIN); "you must register with the Department as a dealer before you begin conducting business activities subject to Florida's taxes." https://floridarevenue.com/Forms_library/current/dr1n.pdf ; register online: https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/sales_tax.aspx (lists "Repairing or altering tangible personal property" as a registrable activity). Reemployment tax is registered on the same DR-1 when triggered.
- **Tint is taxable in full.** Rule 12A-1.006(1)(a): "Where parts are furnished by the repairer, the entire charge the repairer makes to a customer for adjusting, applying, installing, maintaining, remodeling, or repairing tangible personal property is taxable." Film is incorporated into the car → the whole $100 is taxable, even if labor is separately stated. https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-12A-1-006 (rule index: https://www.flrules.org/gateway/ruleno.asp?id=12A-1.006). DOR GT-800067: "Generally, when installing or repairing tangible personal property, parts and labor are taxable. If the job is 'labor only' it is not taxable, but the repairer must document that no parts or other items were incorporated"; "Persons who are in the business of repairing tangible personal property should register as a dealer." https://floridarevenue.com/Forms_library/current/gt800067.pdf
- **Detailing: depends on what stays on the car.** 12A-1.006(15)(a): "The charge for a plain wash job, in which only detergent or water softener is added to the water, is exempt." (15)(b): "The entire charge for a wash job, in which wax, silicones, or any other substance is added that forms a protective film or coating, is taxable." Any wax/sealant/ceramic/tire dressing = whole ticket taxable. 12A-1.006(4): labor-only charges are taxable "unless the repairer (dealer) can establish by evidence in the dealer's records that the dealer furnished no tangible personal property." Practical: buy film/wax tax-free with the DR-13 resale certificate; pay tax on soap/towels/tools (GT-800067: "detergents" are taxable overhead).
- **Rate: 6% state + Broward 1% = 7%.** DR-15DSS 2026 table: Broward 1%, effective Jan 1, 2019, expires **Dec 31, 2048** (not 2026 — I read the table text directly). https://floridarevenue.com/Forms_library/current/dr15dss_26.pdf
- **Pricing rule:** 212.07(2) tax "shall be separately stated as Florida tax on any… invoice"; 212.07(4) a dealer "may not advertise or hold out to the public… that he or she will pay all or any part of the tax" (second-degree misdemeanor). So "$100 + tax" ($107), or $93.46 + $6.54 tax — not "we cover the tax." https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/212.07
- **Filing frequency:** >$1,000 tax/yr monthly; $501–$1,000 quarterly; $101–$500 semiannual; ≤$100 annual; "Most new businesses are set up to file and pay sales and use tax quarterly." Due the 1st, late after the 20th; e-file collection allowance 2.5% of first $1,200 (max $30). https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/sales_tax.aspx
- **EIN:** IRS — you need an EIN if you "Have employees"; "Sole proprietors without employees can use their Social Security Number." Apply online, free, issued immediately; "Beware of websites that charge for this free service." https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/employer-identification-number (apply: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number). Get it now anyway — the SBA bank note and the Coral Springs application ("Federal Tax ID Number") both want it.

## 3. Local business tax receipts (Ch. 205)

- **Broward County LBT — yes for a sole proprietor.** "A Business Tax Receipt (BTR) is required for all businesses operating in Broward County. This includes home based and sole proprietor businesses." "If your business is within city limits, most municipalities require their own receipt before the county BTR can be issued." Register the fictitious name with Sunbiz first. Apply online: https://county-taxes.net/broward/btexpress/apply. Fees $27–$150; issued "within minutes" with documents; renewal opens July 1, expires Sept 30; late: Oct +10%, Nov +15%, Dec +20% + $25, after Dec +25% + $25. https://browardtax.org/business-tax-receipt/ ; https://browardtax.org/faqs/local-business-tax/
- **Broward fee classification (Ordinance 88-35 schedule, Aug 2025 file):** code **322 SERVICE – "CAR DETAILING," 1–5 employees $33.00** ("NO SPECIAL LICENSE NEEDED"); code **277 MANUFACTURING – "Window Tinting," 1–10 employees $45.00** ("Window tinting & electroplating do not need special license"). By contrast, 322C AUTOMOBILE REPAIR and 277C Auto Paint/Body require "STATE MOTOR VEHICLE REPAIR REGISTRATION." https://browardtax.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/LocalBusinessTaxReceiptCategories-1.pdf (whether the county requires both codes for one business: unverified — ask, 954-357-4829 per the FAQ).
- **Coral Springs home-based BTR:** "Any person… that engages in any business… in the City of Coral Springs is required to purchase a Business Tax Receipt" incl. home-based; "If a fictitious name is used, a copy of the registration from the State of Florida must be provided"; expires Sept 30; late 10/15/20/25%; 10% transfer fee for ownership/name change. https://www.coralsprings.gov/Government/Departments/Business-Tax-Office/Business-Tax-Registration. Fee page lists "Most other types of businesses not listed" at **$160.80** (no separate home-based line — unconfirmed for his class). https://www.coralsprings.gov/Government/Departments/Business-Tax-Office
- **Coral Springs home-based application (this is where a mobile detailer gets hurt):** applications "will be reviewed by the Coral Springs Zoning Division and the Police Department"; attachments incl. fictitious name, licenses, and "Current storage agreement for materials and inventory"; notarized affidavit to comply with LDC Ch. 10 §1028, including: "No person other than individuals residing in the dwelling unit shall be engaged in home occupation"; "no display of goods, machinery, equipment nor any performance of work visible or audible from any street"; "No employees or independent contractors of any type shall be permitted at the dwelling unit at any time"; "I cannot store at my residence any materials, supplies or equipment, except telephones, computers, and other office materials… Any required inventory is located at the following address"; violations → receipt revoked. It also warns "many subdivisions in the City have deed restrictions relating to the conduct of a business." https://www.coralsprings.gov/files/assets/public/v/3/documents/business-tax-office/city-of-coral-springs-home-based-business-tax-application.updated_20240424.pdf. City page: "Parking of commercial vehicles at the home is not permitted"; "Employees or independent contractors of the business are not permitted at the dwelling." https://www.coralsprings.gov/Government/Departments/Business-Tax-Office/Home-Based-Business. Vehicle guideline: "Commercial and recreational vehicles… are not permitted to be parked between 9 p.m. and 6 a.m., Monday through Saturday and all-day Sunday." https://www.coralsprings.gov/Government/Departments/Community-Development/Property-Guidelines/Vehicle-and-Parking. Outside storage: driveways/yards "not to be used as storage areas for… cleaning supplies or other items." https://www.coralsprings.gov/Government/Departments/Community-Development/Property-Guidelines/Outside-Storage. Net: Alex cannot meet at the house, film/chemicals/pressure washer need an off-site storage address (a small storage unit), and a lettered van can't sit in the driveway overnight.
- **Other cities (Parkland, Coconut Creek, Margate, Tamarac, Deerfield, Boca):** 205.042 lets a municipality tax only "Any person who maintains a permanent business location or branch office within the municipality" (plus an interstate-commerce catch-all in (3)). Working in a customer's driveway is not a permanent location or branch office, so those cities' receipts are not required; the county receipt covers county-wide activity (205.032: "within its jurisdiction"). Boca Raton is Palm Beach County — no Broward-based obligation there either under the same logic. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/205.042 ; https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/205.032. (205.065's explicit "temporary or transitory" protection applies only to DBPR-regulated trades, so it doesn't cover him; the 205.042 text is what protects him.) https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/205.065
- **Penalty for operating without a receipt:** 205.053(2) — 25% of the tax due plus other penalties; (3) after 150 days' notice, civil action, costs, attorney's fees and up to $250. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/205.053
- **Florida Motor Vehicle Repair Act — real, cheap, ambiguous for tint:** 559.903(7) "motor vehicle repair" = "all maintenance of and modifications and repairs to motor vehicles"; (8) "motor vehicle repair shop" includes "mobile motor vehicle repair shops… self-employed individuals." 559.904: must "register with the department prior to doing business"; fee "1 to 5 employees who perform repairs: $50," renewed biennially ($25 late fee); (6) a mobile shop's certificate shows the owner's home address; (7) "Any person applying for or renewing a local business tax receipt to engage in business as a motor vehicle repair shop must exhibit an active registration certificate… before the local business tax receipt may be issued." 559.920(1): unlawful to "engage… in repair work for compensation… without first being registered." 559.921: customer suits with attorney's fees; FDACS Class I fines up to $1,000/violation (570.971). 559.905: written estimate required when the job "will exceed $150" (not $100 — the old figure). Broward's own schedule treats window tinting as needing no state registration, but the statute's "modifications" language plausibly covers tint; detailing-only is arguably "maintenance." Recommendation: spend the $50 or get FDACS's answer in writing (1-800-HELP-FLA). URLs: https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/559.903 ; /559.904 ; /559.905 ; /559.920 ; /559.921 ; https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/570.971 ; https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Motor-Vehicle-Repair

## 4. Alex (worker) and Rasul (brother)

- **Workers' comp threshold — non-construction:** 440.02(20)(b)2: "All private employments in which four or more employees are employed by the same employer." "Employee" (440.02(18)(a)) is "any person who receives remuneration from an employer for the performance of any work or service" — no full-time qualifier, so part-timers count (the DWC page never uses the words "part-time"; the statute simply has no hours test). https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/440.02
- **Does the owner count himself? No.** 440.02(18)(c)1: a non-construction sole proprietor is an employee only if he "elects to be included… by filing notice." DWC: "Non-construction industry Sole Proprietors… are not employees unless they want to be included… and file a form DWC-251." Threshold page: "Employers with four (4) or more employees, including business owners who are corporate officers or Limited Liability Company (LLC) members, must have workers' compensation coverage." https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/employer/coverage-requirements ; FAQ: "Non-Construction Employers who have 4 or more employees including any non-exempt business owners." https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/employer/education-and-resources/frequently-asked-questions. Ziyad + Alex (+ paid Rasul) = 2 employees → WC not legally required yet.
- **But no WC = no shield.** 440.11(1)(a): if the employer fails to secure compensation, the injured employee "may elect to claim compensation under this chapter or to maintain an action at law"; 440.06: the employer may not defend on fellow-servant negligence, assumption of risk, or comparative negligence. As a sole proprietor that suit lands on Ziyad personally. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/440.11 ; https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/440.06. If coverage is ever required and missing: stop-work order, penalty "2 times the amount the employer would have paid in premium… or $1,000, whichever is greater," $1,000/day for violating a stop-work order (440.107(7)). https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/440.107 ; https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/wc/employer/enforcement
- **Florida reemployment tax:** liable with "at least one quarterly payroll totaling $1,500 or more (including wages for both full and part-time employees)" or "one or more employees for a day… during any 20 weeks in a calendar year"; new-employer rate 2.7% on the first $7,000 per employee (max $189/yr each); RT-6 quarterly; register "by the end of the month following the calendar quarter in which you become an employer"; a sole proprietor's own services are exempt. https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/reemployment.aspx
- **Employee vs. contractor — Alex is almost certainly an employee.** IRS tests: Behavioral ("control… what the worker does and how"), Financial ("how worker is paid, whether expenses are reimbursed, who provides tools/supplies"), Relationship ("is the work performed a key aspect of the business?"); "no 'magic' or set number of factors." Owner's film/tools, owner-set per-job pay, owner's brand, installs are the core service → employee. Consequence: "you may be held liable for employment taxes for that worker"; Form SS-8; Section 530 relief; VCSP. https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee ; https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc762. Federal exposure if misclassified without intent: 26 U.S.C. 3509 — 1.5% of wages for income-tax withholding + 20% of the employee FICA share (3%/40% if no 1099s filed); no cap if "intentional disregard." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/3509. Florida adds back reemployment tax and, if injured, the 440.11/440.06 lawsuit above.
- **Simplest compliant path:** W-2 part-time employee via a payroll service. Verified prices: Patriot Basic $17/mo + $4/worker, Full Service $37/mo + $5/worker (https://www.patriotsoftware.com/payroll/pricing/); Square Payroll $35/mo + $6/person, contractor-only $6/person/mo (https://squareup.com/us/en/payroll/pricing); OnPay $49 + $6/worker (https://onpay.com/pricing). Gusto pricing page blocked (403). Florida has no state income-tax withholding, so payroll is federal + FL reemployment. Employer to-dos: EIN; Form I-9 for "every individual they hire" (https://www.uscis.gov/i-9); Florida new-hire report within 20 days of hire (409.2576(4)(a)); pay ≥ Florida minimum wage — Fla. Const. Art. X §24(c): $1/yr increases "until the Minimum Wage reaches $15.00 per hour on September 30th, 2026" → $14.00 today, **$15.00 from Sept 30, 2026** (four days out). https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Constitution#A10S24
- **1099 route only if genuinely independent** (own tools/film, own pricing, other customers, own insurance): W-9; 1099-NEC when payments are "$2,000 or more" for tax years after 2025, due Jan 31 (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec); Florida still requires new-hire reporting of a contractor paid "$600 or more per calendar year" within 20 days of first payment/contract (409.2576(3)(b), (4)(b)) — Florida's $600 did not move with the federal $2,000. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/409.2576
- **Unpaid "learner" brother:** For a for-profit business the DOL "primary beneficiary" test governs; if the analysis shows he is really an employee, "he or she is entitled to both minimum wage and overtime pay under the FLSA." Factors include whether his work "complements, rather than displaces, the work of paid employees" and provides training "similar to… an educational environment." A brother doing actual customer installs displaces paid work → wage liability risk. https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/71-flsa-internships. FLSA coverage is not just the $500k enterprise test — employees are individually covered if they "regularly make telephone calls to persons located in other States" or handle goods that moved interstate (film ordered from out of state qualifies in practice). https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/14-flsa-coverage. Florida's minimum-wage amendment adopts FLSA definitions (Art. X §24(b)). I found no FLSA family exemption for an adult sibling in a non-farm business. For WC, a true unpaid volunteer is excluded from "employee" (440.02(18)(d)6), so he doesn't count toward four — but an injured Rasul can sue Ziyad personally, and GL policies usually exclude worker injuries (policy-specific; unverified). Cleanest: put him on payroll for the hours he works, or keep him strictly observing, not working.

## 5. Personal-liability reality without an LLC

- **What a claim can reach:** everything Ziyad owns — business and personal bank accounts, vehicles, tools, future income — because there is no separate entity (Next/Progressive statements above; that is the definition of a sole proprietorship). Alex's on-the-job negligence is Ziyad's liability too.
- **Homestead:** Fla. Const. Art. X §4(a)(1): exempt from forced sale "the following property owned by a natural person: (1) a homestead… if located within a municipality, to the extent of one-half acre of contiguous land," with exceptions for taxes, purchase/improvement/repair obligations, and labor performed on the realty; (a)(2) "personal property to the value of one thousand dollars." A judgment can't take a Coral Springs primary residence (≤½ acre) but everything else is fair game. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Constitution#A10S04
- **Head-of-family wage exemption:** 222.11 — "head of family" = one "providing more than one-half of the support for a child or other dependent"; disposable earnings ≤ $750/week fully exempt, >$750/week exempt "unless such person has agreed otherwise in writing"; deposited earnings exempt 6 months if traceable. "Earnings" means "compensation… for personal services or labor whether denominated as wages, salary, commission, or bonus" — whether a sole proprietor's business receipts qualify is contested in Florida courts (my caveat, not a fetched holding); don't plan around it. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/222.11
- **Why insurance is the actual protection now:** a customer's Range Rover paint/electronics claim is paid by general liability + garage keepers (customers' vehicles in your care — coverage definition not verifiable from a fetched page, see blocked list) or by Ziyad. Progressive on detailing: commercial auto is "crucial… for mobile auto detailing businesses because personal auto insurance often doesn't cover vehicles while being used for business purposes"; GL covers "third-party claims, such as property damage and customer injury." https://www.progressivecommercial.com/business-insurance/auto-detailing-insurance/. An LLC would not change this: 605.0304(1) shields a member only from liability "solely by reason of being or acting as a member or manager" — Ziyad's own negligent install stays personal even inside an LLC. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/605.0304
- **What the LLC changes later, and cost:** contract debts, Alex's negligence, and premises-type claims become the company's; Ziyad's personal assets are exposed only for his own acts or a pierced veil. Sunbiz: Articles $100 + registered agent $25 = **$125** required (optional certified copy $30, status $5 → $160 with all extras); annual report **$138.75** due Jan 1–May 1, **$400 late fee** after May 1, administrative dissolution if not filed by the third Friday of September. Move "Car Mommy" to the LLC via cancel + re-register ($50). https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/start-business/efile/fl-llc/ ; https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/manage-business/efile/annual-report/ ; https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/forms/fees/

## 6. Anything else for driveway tint/detailing

- **Tint legality is on the installer, criminally.** 316.2956: "Any person who sells or installs sunscreening material in violation of any provision of ss. 316.2951-316.2955 is guilty of a misdemeanor of the second degree." Limits: front side windows ≥28% VLT, ≤25% reflectance (316.2953); behind the driver ≥15% (6% multipurpose), ≤35% reflectance (316.2954); ±3% tolerance. 316.2955: "Each such installer shall affix the required label to the inside left door jamb" — a pressure-sensitive nonremovable vinyl label stating compliance, "the trade name of the material and the installer's or seller's business name." Print "Car Mommy" jamb labels before job one. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/316.2953 ; /316.2954 ; /316.2955 ; /316.2956
- **HOA / gated communities:** no Florida statute requires written consent to work on a customer's driveway; the customer's invitation covers entry. Deed restrictions and condo rules routinely bar vehicle work/washing on-site (Coral Springs' own application warns about deed restrictions). Put a one-line "customer confirms work at this address is permitted by any HOA/condo rules" clause in the booking form; that's practice, not law (no citation).
- **Water/runoff:** Coral Springs stormwater page: "Soaps used to wash cars also contain ingredients known to harm aquatic wildlife"; "Storm drains… do not go to wastewater treatment plants"; "Wash your car on your lawn instead of the driveway/street or go to a local car wash." No illicit-discharge ordinance number captured. Use waterless/rinseless products or a mat with capture, and don't let rinse water hit the curb. https://www.coralsprings.gov/Government/Departments/Public-Works/Stormwater
- **Noise:** Code §11-11 (2016 copy; verify current text on Municode): general ban on "any loud or raucous noise so as to disturb the peace, quiet or comfort of the adjacent neighborhood" (11-11(11)); machinery "attended by loud or raucous noise" barred 6 p.m.–7 a.m. weekdays and 6 p.m.–9 a.m. weekends/holidays (11-11(12)); landscape-type equipment exempt 7 a.m.–8 p.m. weekdays / 9 a.m.–8 p.m. weekends if muffled (11-12(5)). Practical window for pressure washer/vac/blower: 9 a.m.–6 p.m. https://www.nonoise.org/lawlib/cities/ordinances/Coral%20Springs,%20Florida.pdf (current: https://library.municode.com/fl/coral_springs/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICO_CH11OFIS_S11-11NODI — JS page, not fetched)
- **.us domain:** usTLD Nexus Category 1 — "A natural person (i) who is a United States citizen, (ii) who is a permanent resident… or (iii) whose primary place of domicile is in the United States"; "continuing requirement"; failure → 30-day hold then cancellation, no refund. A sole proprietor qualifies as an individual registrant; no entity needed. https://www.about.us/documents/policies/usTLD_Nexus_Requirements_Policy.pdf
- **Google Business Profile:** name "should reflect your business's real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers" — the DBA registration and BTRs are the paper for "Car Mommy"; as a service-area business he must hide the home address and set a service area (≈2-hour drive max). https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177

## Where the no-LLC choice actually bites you

1. Every claim — a ruined interior, a scratched windshield, Alex rear-ending someone on the way to a job — is against Ziyad personally; the house (≤½ acre) survives, the savings, vehicles, and tools don't. Insurance limits are the only real wall.
2. Alex gets hurt → with no WC policy (not required under 4 employees) he can sue Ziyad personally and Ziyad can't plead comparative negligence (440.11, 440.06). Consider a voluntary WC policy or at least confirm the GL policy's employee-injury exclusion.
3. Unpaid Rasul doing real installs is wage exposure (FLSA/Art. X §24) and uninsured injury exposure, all personal.
4. Coral Springs home-occupation rules are built for a home office: no employees/contractors at the house, no equipment or supplies stored there, no commercial vehicle parked overnight. The LLC doesn't fix this, but the "lean" plan must budget an off-site storage address or the city receipt can be revoked.
5. The DBA gives no name rights — anyone can register or use "Car Mommy" (865.09(8)); brand protection is trademark, not Sunbiz.
6. Conversion later is cheap but fiddly: LLC $125 + DBA cancel/re-register $50 + new EIN + new DOR account + Coral Springs 10% transfer fee + re-papering bank/insurance/GBP; do it before signing anything recurring (leases, fleet insurance, financing).
7. What the LLC does NOT change: Ziyad's own negligent install remains his personal liability (605.0304 "solely by reason of being… a member"); sales tax, LBTs, FDACS, tint law and payroll obligations are identical either way.
