Research date 2026-09-26. Web-search budget was exhausted at 200 queries; everything below is from fetched pages unless marked **(snippet only)** = seen in a search result but the page itself was not opened, or **(unverified)**. Blocked sources are listed in section (d).

## 1. Formats that convert for local car-service accounts

**Hard numbers (platform-wide, not detailing-specific)**
- Socialinsider 2026 benchmark (35M posts, 447,613 pages, Jan–Dec 2025): engagement rate Q2 2026 — Reels 0.48%, carousels 0.50%, images 0.33%. Carousels also out-save Reels (98 vs 96 avg saves) and, for 100K–1M accounts, out-view them (35,370 vs 16,035 avg views). Platform ER is down 24% YoY. https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram
- Takeaway: Reels are the discovery tool (non-followers), carousels are the "save/compare/decide" tool. A launch account with ~0 followers needs Reels for reach first; carousels for pricing/FAQ so people save them.
- Hootsuite Reels-for-business guide (Mar 17, 2026): hook in the first seconds, "don't use title cards or slow intros"; Reels are "often viewed sound-off first" so on-screen text is mandatory; ranking signals are watch time, completion, saves, shares and post-view actions (profile visits). Recommended formats explicitly include before/afters and customer interviews. https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-reels/

**Industry (detailing/tint) consensus — all qualitative, none publish booking data**
- Undrdog (detailing-chemical brand): Instagram 3–5 feed posts/week + daily Stories; "consistency matters more than frequency"; before/after is "the easiest way to really 'wow' your audience"; add a location tag to every post; up to 20 hashtags. https://getundrdog.com/blogs/detailing-business-resources/social-media-for-detailers-everything-you-need-to-know
- Detail King marketing playbook: Facebook 2–3×/week with before/after, promos, testimonials; join local Facebook groups; hashtag pattern "#[YourCity]Detailer". https://blog.detailking.com/marketing-guide-for-detailers/
- Jobber (detailing advertising guide): before/after photos, time-lapse Reels; "join or start a Facebook Group for car enthusiasts in your working area"; one detailer reports "$1 a day" in Facebook ads producing "two to three customers a month"; flyers at "local auto shops, tire centers, and car washes"; dealership cross-promos. https://www.getjobber.com/academy/auto-detailing/car-detailing-advertising/
- Socialmon (60 detailing post ideas): "Before and after content is the single best format"; "Interior transformations are your most shareable content"; "Pet hair, coffee stains, food debris — the messier the before, the more engagement you get. Parents and pet owners see themselves in it."; "Client pickup reaction moment" is "the most emotionally powerful content you can post"; also recommends a "How we price our services" transparency post. No metrics. https://www.socialmon.ai/blog/60-car-detailing-instagram-post-ideas-with-examples
- Tint Wiz (tint-shop software blog): before/after must be "from the same angle, in similar lighting"; "videos of installations or before-and-after shots performing best." https://tintwiz.com/window-tint-marketing/how-to-market-your-window-tinting-business and https://tintwiz.com/window-tint-marketing/how-to-market-a-new-window-tint-business **(snippet only)**
- Snow-foam / paint-correction / myth-busting Reels called out as top detailing formats **(snippet only, source page not opened)**.

**Cadence (Instagram's own guidance)**
- Adam Mosseri: "brands should aim to post a couple of Stories each day"; a couple of feed posts per week as baseline. Reported via Socialchamp (no direct link to Mosseri's post). https://www.socialchamp.com/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-instagram/
- Practical solo-operator target that matches all sources: 3 Reels + 1 carousel per week, 1–3 Stories every working day.

**What I could NOT find data for:** talking-head pricing/FAQ clips, customer-reaction clips, or local-landmark shots as measured booking drivers. Closest evidence: SetSmart's 373K-Instagram-conversation dataset shows bookings come from DM depth — "53% of Instagram conversations die before message 3"; booking rate 0.07% at 1–4 messages vs 2.1% at 5–10 and 11.25% at 11–20; impression-to-booking ≈0.04%. Pricing/FAQ content is valuable because it starts price DMs, which is where bookings actually happen. https://setsmart.io/blog/instagram-lead-generation (vendor's own data)

**Blunt read:** for a driveway service, the transition before/after Reel (same angle, same light, 5–8 s, on-screen text with city name + price) is the single format every industry source agrees on. Everything else is support.

- Meta's own help page ("Use Location Targeting") would not render (error page). https://www.facebook.com/business/help/365561350785642 — **blocked**.
- Secondary sources agree: pin/address radius minimum 1 mile, max 50 miles in the US; four location types (living in or recently in / living in / recently in / traveling in). Coinis (cites Meta docs): https://coinis.com/how-to/radius-targeting-facebook-ads
- CONFLICT to check in your own Ads Manager: Bigeye (Sep 26, 2026) says "as of 2026… only 'Living in or recently in this location' is available" and Advantage+ treats a radius "as a preference signal", not a hard wall. https://www.bigeyeagency.com/insights/geographic-targeting-for-meta-ads-guide-2026 — while Stackmatix (Sep 9, 2026) still recommends selecting "People living in this location" and a 3–5 mile radius for service businesses, $500/month minimum viable, "$1,000–$2,000/month gives you enough data." https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/local-facebook-ads-targeting
- Thread Transfer (Jun 17, 2026) claims sub-5-mile radii get CPM-penalized and Advantage+ silently expands 1-mile sets; it cites no Meta doc — **unverified**. https://thread-transfer.com/blog/2026-06-17-meta-ads-location-targeting-radius-minimum/

**Lead form vs "Send message"**
- Meta's own pages state (quoted in search results; pages login-walled for me): multiple message destinations → "9% lower cost per message and 11% higher conversion"; instant forms + Messenger destination → "8% lower cost per lead and 48% increase in reach" vs instant forms alone. https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/click-to-message-ads and https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/ad-objectives/lead-generation/lead-ads-with-messaging — **snippet only**.

**Nextdoor vs Facebook groups vs Instagram**

Qualitative consensus: Instagram = proof of work; FB groups/Nextdoor = where "anyone know a good mobile tint guy?" gets asked. Do both: free Nextdoor business page (Coral Springs + Parkland neighborhoods), and ask each $100 customer to post their before/after in their own community group (their post, not yours — most groups ban business self-promo).

## 4. Review flywheel and Google's rule

**Policy (cite these)**
- Google Maps UGC policy, section "Rating Manipulation": prohibited content includes "Content that has been posted due to an incentive offered by a business - such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services." https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en
- Google Business Profile help: "Offering incentives, like free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers to post reviews, change reviews, or remove negative reviews is considered fake & misleading content and is strictly prohibited." Same page: "ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code" (link/QR generator is in the profile's review-request tool, help article 16816815). https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en
- April 2026 update (secondary summary): Google added bans on review quotas, asking for specific content or staff names, requiring reviews on premises, and gating; enforcement escalates from removal → temporary block on new reviews → public warning banner → suspension. https://launchcodex.com/blog/seo-geo-ai/google-business-profile-review-policy-update/
- FTC rule (announced Aug 14, 2024; effective 60 days after FR publication, i.e., Oct 21, 2024): "prohibits businesses from providing compensation or other incentives conditioned on the writing of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, either positive or negative"; FTC can "seek civil penalties against knowing violators." https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials (the $51,744-per-violation figure appears only in secondary sources — **unverified** here.)

**How to position $100 so it is NOT a review bribe**
- $100 is a public, unconditional "launch price" for the first N cars — same price whether or not they review, and you never say "review for the price." Say the reason out loud: "new installer building a portfolio, first 50 cars at $100."
- The review ask is separate, neutral, sent to every customer (no gating), no "5-star" wording, no request to mention names.

**Timing/mechanics (vendor claims, no independent data)**
- Epicware: car workshops — ask "1–2 hours after vehicle pickup"; "conversion rates drop by roughly 60% after 24 hours" (unsourced). https://www.epicware.ai/blog/best-time-to-ask-for-a-google-review
- Textline: SMS "98 percent open rate and a 45 percent response rate" vs email 20%/6% (unsourced); service businesses should ask same day. https://www.textline.com/blog/ask-for-google-reviews
- QR-card conversion claims (37% CTR; 4%→22%) — **snippet only, unverified**.
- Flywheel that fits a driveway job: (1) at handoff, show the result and hand a QR card that opens the Google review link; (2) 1–2 hours later, text the link; (3) 48 h later one follow-up; (4) screenshot every review into a Story and a monthly "reviews" carousel.

## 5. Brand angle — what the evidence supports

**UV / skin angle (strongest, citable)**
- Skin Cancer Foundation: side, back and sunroof glass "does not block UVA rays effectively"; "Up to 50 percent of UVA radiation can penetrate this glass"; US skin cancers are "more common on the left side" because of driver-side exposure; Seal of Recommendation requires film that blocks ≥99% UVA/UVB. https://www.skincancer.org/skin-cancer-prevention/sun-protection/uv-window-film/ and https://www.skincancer.org/blog/driving-your-risk-for-skin-cancer/
- 2025 peer-reviewed test (Archives of Dermatological Research, 34 vehicles MY2015–2025): windshields attenuate 99.25% of UV-A vs driver-side windows 88.78% (p<0.001), rear side windows 93.18% — i.e., the back-seat windows where kids sit are the weakest glass. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11742901/
- Hook: "Your windshield blocks 99% of UVA. The window next to your kid's car seat blocks ~89–93%. Tint closes the gap."

**Heat angle (weak public data — make your own)**
- No independent cabin-temperature study of tinted vs untinted cars found. Tint-shop tests only: CoolVu "30-day Florida" 159°F vs 127°F **(snippet only; page blank)**; Winter Park Tint's Orlando test reports no numbers https://www.winterparktint.com/orlando-summer-tint-test ; Carteks' "20–40°F lower" is unsourced https://www.carteks.com/window-tint-reduce-heat . Don't quote these numbers. Film an IR-thermometer test on your own two cars in a Coral Springs parking lot; that clip is worth more than any stat.
- NWS: "in just over 2 minutes the car went from a safe temperature to an unsafe temperature of 94.3°F"; average 38 child heatstroke deaths/year (1998–2022). https://www.weather.gov/safety/heat-children-pets — use only as "cooler cabin, cooler car seat buckles" messaging; do not imply tint prevents heatstroke.
- EPA UV Index: 8+ = "Very High to Extreme." https://www.epa.gov/sunsafety/uv-index-scale-0 (Florida-specific averages not verified.)

**Florida legal hook (statute text)**
- §316.2953: front side windows ≥28% VLT, ≤25% reflectance. §316.2954: behind the driver ≥15% (sedans) / ≥6% (multipurpose/SUV), ≤35% reflectance; a non-transparent rear window requires both side mirrors. http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.2953.html and …/0316.2954.html
- Content: "SUV moms get to go 6% in the back — legally." That's a real differentiator vs sedan owners and a great explainer Reel.

**Seasonal detailing hooks**
- Lovebugs (UF/IFAS): flights peak "about four weeks in April through May and August through September"; "their body fluids are slightly acid… the damage to the finish occurs with time… acidity… etches the paint." https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/wakullaco/files/2012/09/LoveBugs.pdf . 2026 season was bigger than recent years; car-wash lines "super long" (WUFT, Jun 3, 2026). https://www.wuft.org/environment/2026-06-03/north-central-florida-car-washes-combat-lovebug-cleanup (Note: NW Broward gets lighter swarms than central FL; still a May/Sept hook.)
- Hurricane season June 1–Nov 30, peak Sept 10 (NOAA/NHC). https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/climo/ — hooks: "pre-storm interior/leather protection", "post-storm mildew/water-intrusion extraction".
- Sap/pollen/salt-air: no Florida-specific data found; use as generic hooks only.

**Family/mom angle**
- Direct evidence is thin: no Florida tint/detail case study with a family angle surfaced. Indirect: Socialmon's "Parents and pet owners see themselves in it" (above); WD Detailing's "Deep Cleaning a Mom's DISASTER Minivan!" exists as a genre https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJwdWXVvims (view count blocked — 429); and the two large local mom groups listed in §2 are the distribution channel. 1. Transition before/after of a tint in a recognizable local driveway/HOA street, city + $100 on screen — the format every source agrees on; the price is the hook in a market whose floor is $139–$249.

4. Kid-mess SUV interior extraction reveal — most shareable detailing content per industry sources; mothers tag mothers.
5. "$100 vs shop price" explainer — plain talking head, why it's $100 (new installer, portfolio, first 50 cars) — converts because it removes the "what's the catch" objection that kills DMs.
6. Customer handoff reaction (permission-based) — "most emotionally powerful" per industry guides; local face = local trust.
7. FL legal darkness explainer (28/15/6, statute on screen) — high saves; SUV owners learn they can go 6% in back.
