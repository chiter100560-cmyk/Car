# Florida window-tint statutes (ss. 316.2951–316.2957): primary-source verification, labeling vendors, enforcement reality

**Method / sources.** All eight statutes were fetched from the Florida Legislature's Online Sunshine site (leg.state.fl.us), whose pages are headed **"The 2026 Florida Statutes"**, and cross-checked against flsenate.gov's 2025 and 2026 pages (identical text). The proxy worked; no TLS/403 issues on the primary sites. FLHSMV form HSMV 83390, procedure RS-48, FHP Policies 17.27, 17.06 and 6.03 were downloaded as PDFs and text-extracted. Web-search budget (200 queries) was exhausted near the end; see §6 for what could not be reached.

Note on quotation marks: the statute prints each defined term in quotation marks (e.g., "Motor vehicle" means…); the fetch tool's text conversion rendered those marks inconsistently, so the definitions below are shown with the defined term in quotes as the official page prints them. Everything else is verbatim as returned.

## 1. Verbatim statute text (2026 Florida Statutes)

### 316.2951 — Definitions
URL: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.2951.html (2026); cross-check https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/316.2951 (2025, identical)

> **316.2951 Motor vehicle windows; definitions.**—Whenever used in ss. 316.2951-316.2957, unless the context otherwise requires, the following terms have the following meanings:
> (1) "Motor vehicle" means any vehicle as defined in s. 316.003, except vehicles used in farm husbandry, which is registered or required to be registered in the state.
> (2) "Multipurpose passenger vehicle" means a motor vehicle with motive power designed to carry 10 persons or fewer which is constructed either on a truck chassis or with special features for occasional off-road operation.
> (3) "Reflectance" means the ratio of the amount of total light, expressed in a percentage, which is reflected outward by the product or material to the amount of total light falling on the product or material.
> (4) "Sunscreening material" means a product or material, including film, glazing, and perforated sunscreening, which, when applied to the windshield or windows of a motor vehicle, reduces the effects of the sun with respect to light reflectance or transmittance.
> (5) "Transmittance" means the ratio of the amount of total light, expressed in a percentage, which is allowed to pass through the product or material, including glazing, to the amount of total light falling on the product or material and the glazing.
> (6) "Window" means any device designed for exterior viewing from a motor vehicle, except the windshield, any roof-mounted viewing device, and any viewing device having less than 150 square inches in area.
> (7) "Windshield" means the front exterior viewing device of a motor vehicle.
> History.—s. 1, ch. 84-296.

Key reading: the statute never says "SUV/truck/van"; the 6% class is "multipurpose passenger vehicle" as defined in (2) (≤10 persons AND built on a truck chassis or with special features for occasional off-road operation). Transmittance is measured through film + glass together ("including glazing").

### 316.2952 — Windshields
URL: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.2952.html (2026)

> **316.2952 Windshields; requirements; restrictions.**—
> (1) A windshield in a fixed and upright position, which windshield is equipped with safety glazing as required by federal safety-glazing material standards, is required on every motor vehicle which is operated on the public highways, roads, and streets, except on a motorcycle or implement of husbandry.
> (2) A person shall not operate any motor vehicle on any public highway, road, or street with any sign, sunscreening material, product, or covering attached to, or located in or upon, the windshield, except the following:
> (a) A certificate or other paper required to be displayed by law.
> (b) Sunscreening material along a strip at the top of the windshield, so long as such material is transparent and does not encroach upon the driver's direct forward viewing area as more particularly described and defined in Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards No. 205 as the AS/1 portion of the windshield.
> (c) A device, issued by a governmental entity as defined in s. 334.03, or its designee, for the purpose of electronic toll payments.
> (d) A global positioning system device or similar satellite receiver device that uses the global positioning system operated pursuant to 10 U.S.C. s. 2281 to obtain navigation, to improve driver safety as a component of safety monitoring equipment capable of providing driver feedback, or to otherwise route information while the motor vehicle is being operated.
> (3) The windshield on every motor vehicle shall be equipped with a device for cleaning rain, snow, or other moisture from the windshield, which device shall be constructed as to be controlled or operated by the driver of the vehicle.
> (4) Every windshield wiper upon a motor vehicle shall be maintained in good working order.
> (5) Grove equipment, including "goats," "highlift-goats," grove chemical supply tanks, fertilizer distributors, fruit-loading equipment, and electric-powered vehicles regulated under the provisions of s. 316.267, are exempt from the requirements of this section. However, such electric-powered vehicles shall have a windscreen approved by the department sufficient to give protection from wind, rain, or insects, and such windscreen shall be in place whenever the vehicle is operated on the public roads and highways.
> (6) A former military vehicle is exempt from the requirements of this section if the department determines that the exemption is necessary to maintain the vehicle's accurate military design and markings. However, whenever the vehicle is operating on the public roads and highways, the operator and passengers must wear eye-protective devices approved by the department. For purposes of this subsection, "former military vehicle" means a vehicle, including a trailer, regardless of the vehicle's size, weight, or year of manufacture, that was manufactured for use in any country's military forces and is maintained to represent its military design and markings accurately.
> (7) A violation of this section is a noncriminal traffic infraction, punishable as a nonmoving violation as provided in chapter 318.
> History.—s. 1, ch. 84-296; s. 15, ch. 93-164; s. 208, ch. 99-248; s. 1, ch. 2003-286; s. 1, ch. 2005-47; s. 8, ch. 2010-223; s. 13, ch. 2014-216.

### 316.2953 — Side windows (front)
URL: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.2953.html (2026); cross-check https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/316.2953 (same numbers)

> **316.2953 Side windows; restrictions on sunscreening material.**—A person shall not operate any motor vehicle on any public highway, road, or street on which vehicle the side wings and side windows on either side forward of or adjacent to the operator's seat are composed of, covered by, or treated with any sunscreening material or other product or covering which has the effect of making the window nontransparent or which would alter the window's color, increase its reflectivity, or reduce its light transmittance, except as expressly permitted by this section. A sunscreening material is authorized for such windows if, when applied to and tested on the glass of such windows on the specific motor vehicle, the material has a total solar reflectance of visible light of not more than 25 percent as measured on the nonfilm side and a light transmittance of at least 28 percent in the visible light range. A violation of this section is a noncriminal traffic infraction, punishable as a nonmoving violation as provided in chapter 318.
> History.—s. 1, ch. 84-296; s. 1, ch. 91-42; s. 209, ch. 99-248.

Key reading: front sides = **≥28% VLT, ≤25% reflectance**, measured on the specific vehicle's glass with film applied. (Page returned as one unnumbered paragraph.)

### 316.2954 — Windows behind the driver
URL: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.2954.html (2026); cross-check https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/316.2954 (35% confirmed)

> **316.2954 Windows behind the driver; restrictions on sunscreening material.**—
> (1) A person shall not operate any motor vehicle on any public highway, road, or street on which vehicle any windows behind the driver are composed of, covered by, or treated with any sunscreening material, or other product or material which has the effect of making the window nontransparent or which would alter the window's color, increase its reflectivity, or reduce its light transmittance, except as specified below:
> (a) Sunscreening material consisting of film which, when applied to and tested on the rear window glass of the specific motor vehicle, has a total solar reflectance of visible light of not more than 35 percent as measured on the nonfilm side and a light transmittance of at least 15 percent in the visible light range; however, sunscreening material which, when applied to and tested on the rear window glass of the specific motor vehicle, has a total solar reflectance of visible light of not more than 35 percent as measured on the nonfilm side and a light transmittance of at least 6 percent in the visible light range may be used on multipurpose passenger vehicles.
> (b) Perforated sunscreening material which, when tested in conjunction with existing glazing or film material, has a total reflectance of visible light of not more than 35 percent and a light transmittance of no less than 30 percent. For those products or materials having different levels of reflectance, the highest reflectance from the product or material will be measured by dividing the area into 16 equal sections and averaging the overall reflectance. The measured reflectance of any of those sections may not exceed 50 percent.
> (c) Louvered materials, if the installation of the materials does not reduce driver visibility by more than 50 percent.
> (d) Privacy drapes, curtains and blinds, provided such covering is in an open and secure position when the motor vehicle is being operated on any public highway, road, or street.
> (2) A person shall not operate any motor vehicle upon any public highway, road, or street, on which vehicle the rear window is composed of, covered by, or treated with any material which has the effect of making the window nontransparent, unless the vehicle is equipped with side mirrors on both sides that meet the requirements of s. 316.294.
> (3) A violation of this section is a noncriminal traffic infraction, punishable as a nonmoving violation as provided in chapter 318.
> History.—s. 1, ch. 84-296; s. 2, ch. 91-42; s. 210, ch. 99-248.

Key reading: behind the driver the reflectance cap is **35%, not 25%**; VLT ≥15% (≥6% on multipurpose passenger vehicles); perforated film ≥30% VLT; dual side mirrors required if the rear window is nontransparent.

### 316.29545 — Medical exemption; law-enforcement, process-server and PI vehicles
URL: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.29545.html (2026)

> **316.29545 Window sunscreening exclusions; medical exemption; certain law enforcement vehicles, process server vehicles, and private investigative service vehicles exempt.**—
> (1) The department shall issue medical exemption certificates to persons who are afflicted with Lupus, any autoimmune disease, or other medical conditions which require a limited exposure to light, which certificates shall entitle the person to whom the certificate is issued to have sunscreening material on the windshield, side windows, and windows behind the driver which is in violation of the requirements of ss. 316.2951-316.2957. The department shall consult with the Medical Advisory Board established in s. 322.125 for guidance with respect to the autoimmune diseases and other medical conditions which shall be included on the form of the medical certificate authorized by this section. At a minimum, the medical exemption certificate shall include a vehicle description with the make, model, year, vehicle identification number, medical exemption decal number issued for the vehicle, and the name of the person or persons who are the registered owners of the vehicle. A medical exemption certificate shall be nontransferable and shall become null and void upon the sale or transfer of the vehicle identified on the certificate.
> (2) The department shall exempt all law enforcement vehicles used in undercover or canine operations from the window sunscreening requirements of ss. 316.2951-316.2957.
> (3) The department shall exempt from the window sunscreening restrictions of ss. 316.2953, 316.2954, and 316.2956 vehicles that are owned or leased by process servers certified pursuant to s. 48.29 or by private investigators or private investigative agencies licensed under chapter 493.
> (4) The department may charge a fee in an amount sufficient to defray the expenses of issuing a medical exemption certificate as described in subsection (1).
> History.—s. 14, ch. 2000-313; s. 9, ch. 2010-223; s. 10, ch. 2019-5; s. 8, ch. 2019-67.

Key reading: the law-enforcement / PI / process-server exemptions live here, not in 316.2957. Note (3) exempts PI/process-server vehicles from 316.2953, 316.2954 and the penalty section 316.2956, but does not list the 316.2955 labeling section.

### 316.2955 — Labeling; tolerance; approved meters
URL: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.2955.html (2026); identical at https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/316.2955 and https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/316.2955

> **316.2955 Window sunscreening material; compliance labeling; tolerances.**—
> (1) Each installer or seller of sunscreening material shall provide a pressure-sensitive, self-destructive, nonremovable, vinyl-type film label to the purchaser stating that the material complies with the provisions of ss. 316.2951-316.2954. Each such installer shall affix the required label to the inside left door jamb of the motor vehicle. In addition, the label shall state the trade name of the material and the installer's or seller's business name. Labeling is not required for factory glazing which complies with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 205.
> (2) Every percentage measurement required by ss. 316.2951-316.2954 is subject to a tolerance of plus or minus 3 percent.
> (3) The department shall adopt rules approving light transmittance measuring devices for use in making measurements required by ss. 316.2951-316.2954. A witness otherwise qualified to testify shall be competent to give testimony regarding the percentage of light transmission when the testimony is derived from the use of an approved device. The reading from an approved device is presumed accurate and shall be admissible into evidence in the trial of any infraction arising under ss. 316.2951-316.2954.
> History.—s. 1, ch. 84-296; s. 24, ch. 94-306.

Element-by-element:
- **Who provides:** "Each installer or seller" must provide the label "to the purchaser."
- **Who affixes:** "Each such installer shall affix" it — the affixing duty is on the installer only.
- **Material:** "pressure-sensitive, self-destructive, nonremovable, vinyl-type film label" (statute says "vinyl-type film", not simply "vinyl").
- **Where:** exact wording is **"the inside left door jamb of the motor vehicle"** — "left door jamb" is the statute's own phrase (not "driver's door jamb").
- **What it must say (three things):** (a) that the material "complies with the provisions of ss. 316.2951-316.2954"; (b) "the trade name of the material"; (c) "the installer's or seller's business name."
- **Exception:** factory glazing meeting FMVSS 205.
- **Tolerance:** ±3 percent on "every percentage measurement" (statutory; the FAC and FHP treat it as ±3 percentage points of meter accuracy — see §2).
- No size, color, font, VLT figure, date, or license number is required by statute.

### 316.2956 — Penalties
URL: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.2956.html (2026); identical at https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/316.2956

> **316.2956 Violation of provisions relating to windshields, windows, and sunscreening material; penalties.**—
> (1) Any person who operates a motor vehicle on which, after June 20, 1984, material was installed in violation of ss. 316.2951-316.2954 commits a noncriminal traffic infraction, punishable as a nonmoving violation as provided in chapter 318.
> (2) The replacement or repair of any material legally installed is not a violation of ss. 316.2951-316.2954.
> (3) Any person who sells or installs sunscreening material in violation of any provision of ss. 316.2951-316.2955 is guilty of a misdemeanor of the second degree, punishable as provided in s. 775.082 or s. 775.083.
> History.—s. 1, ch. 84-296; s. 15, ch. 86-185; s. 211, ch. 99-248.

Who commits what:
- **Driver/operator** (sub. (1)): noncriminal infraction, nonmoving violation — only for material installed in violation of **316.2951–316.2954** (the label section 316.2955 is not included, so a driver is not cited for a missing label). Base penalty: s. 318.18(2) — "Thirty dollars for all nonmoving traffic violations and:" (2026; plus statutory add-ons/court costs) — http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0318/Sections/0318.18.html
- **Seller or installer** (sub. (3)): second-degree misdemeanor for violating **any provision of 316.2951–316.2955** — this includes failing to provide/affix the 316.2955 label, and it reaches sellers as well as installers. s. 775.082(4)(b) (2026): "For a misdemeanor of the second degree, by a definite term of imprisonment not exceeding 60 days." s. 775.083(1)(e) (2026): "$500, when the conviction is of a misdemeanor of the second degree or a noncriminal violation." (URLs: …/0700-0799/0775/Sections/0775.082.html and …/0775.083.html on leg.state.fl.us)

### 316.2957 — Manufacturer exemption
URL: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.2957.html (2026)

> **316.2957 Exemption for motor vehicle manufacturers.**—The provisions of ss. 316.2951-316.2956 do not apply to the manufacturer's tinting or glazing of motor vehicle windows or windshields which is otherwise in compliance with or permitted by Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 205 as promulgated in 49 C.F.R. s. 571.205.
> History.—s. 1, ch. 84-296.

Key reading: 316.2957 is only the OEM-glazing exemption. Law enforcement / PI / process-server exemptions are in 316.29545(2)–(3).

### Colors — what the text actually says
None of ss. 316.2951–316.2957 names any color. There is no "red, amber, blue" list. Both 316.2953 and 316.2954 prohibit any material "which has the effect of making the window nontransparent or which would alter the window's color, increase its reflectivity, or reduce its light transmittance, except as expressly permitted" — and the only express permission is material meeting the reflectance/transmittance numbers. Secondary sites that list banned colors (e.g., TintLaws' reference card: "No red, amber, or other colored tint that resembles emergency-vehicle or traffic-signal colors is permitted") are not quoting the statute.

## 2. Administrative rules, FHP guidance, FLHSMV medical-exemption process

**F.A.C. Chapter 15B-14, "Light Transmittance Measuring Devices"** (flrules.org chapter page confirms two rules, both effective 1/2/1996: 15B-14.001 Approval Requirements; 15B-14.002 Requirements for Type Acceptance — https://flrules.org/gateway/ChapterHome.asp?Chapter=15B-14 ; rule page https://www.flrules.org/gateway/ruleNo.asp?id=15B-14.002 shows "Rulemaking Authority 316.2955 FS. Law Implemented 316.2951-316.2956 FS. History–New 1-2-96" but the rule text is only in a downloadable doc). Text taken from Cornell LII's copy (secondary): 15B-14.002 requires the light source be "mid-range in the visible spectrum (560 nanometers plus or minus 20 nanometers)", devices must "maintain unit accuracy within plus or minus three percentage points" with "repeatability within plus or minus one percentage point from reading to reading", reference samples "glass or plastic based with a thickness of 1/8 inch to 1/4 inch and stable for at least one year" and "recalibrated at least once per year." 15B-14.001: manufacturers submit "an original report by an independent testing laboratory qualified to do business in Florida certifying that each model performs to factory specifications" to the "Bureau of Law Enforcement Support Services, Division of Florida Highway Patrol". (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-15B-14-002 and …-001)

**FLHSMV approved window tint meters** (https://www.flhsmv.gov/florida-highway-patrol/useful-info-links/approved-window-tint-meters/): Laser Lab 100-200-400; Monroe PMP Pocket Detective Window Tint Meter; New World Instruments Pocket Detective 2.1; Tint Meter Model 1000 (Enforcer II) and Model 2000 (Inspector II). (Patrol cars may use only "3M Color Stable 35", "Llumar ATX40", and "Llumar AT35".)

**Medical exemption — current form and process**
- Form: **HSMV 83390, "Application for Sunscreening Medical Exemption," Rev. 07/25** — https://www.flhsmv.gov/pdf/forms/83390.pdf (listed on https://www.flhsmv.gov/resources/forms/). No stand-alone FLHSMV HTML page was found; the form and the procedure are the official sources.
- Procedure: **RS-48 "Sunscreening Medical Exemption Certificate," Revision Date 05/2025** (formerly TL-55; renamed 02/24) — https://www.flhsmv.gov/pdf/proc/rs/rs-48.pdf
- Mail to (form): "Bureau of Motorist Compliance, 2900 Apalachee Pkwy, Mail Stop 97, Tallahassee, FL 32399." (RS-48: "Room A201 MS# 97, Neil Kirkman Building, 2900 Apalachee Parkway".) "FLHSMV processes all sun screening request transactions."
- Conditions on the form: "☐Lupus, ☐ Dermatomyositis, ☐ Albinism, ☐ Total or Facial Vitiligo, ☐ Xeroderma Pigmentosum, ☐other Autoimmune Disease or other medical condition which requires a limited exposure to light" (physician section adds "(with positive ANA titer)" for Lupus and Dermatomyositis).
- Who signs the "Physician's Statement of Certification" (form, Rev. 07/25): "Physician licensed to practice under Chapters 458, 459, or 460, Florida Statutes"; "Dermatologist licensed to practice under Chapter 458"; a physician in "a military medical facility, state hospital or federal prison"; "An advanced registered nurse practitioner licensed under Chapter 464, under the protocol of a licensed physician"; "Physician assistant licensed under chapter 458 or 459". (RS-48 also lists the VA Adjudication Office.)
- ID: form requires "A photocopy of a current Florida Driver License" or ID card; RS-48 accepts out-of-state DL/ID, passports, Canadian documents and states "Customer does not have to have a Florida driver license or ID card. If vehicle is titled and registered in Florida, it may have sun screening material applied."
- Fee: form says "Fees for each applicable vehicle: $ 6.50"; RS-48 says "$6.45" (discrepancy between the two current documents — flag it). Duplicate same fee; lost-in-transit replacement free within 180 days.
- Certificate: "The form HSMV 83391, Sunscreening Medical Exemption Certificate, is always issued to the registered owner of the motor vehicle" (who "may or may not be the person with the medical condition"); "Only one medical exemption certificate may be issued for each motor vehicle"; it "must be kept in the vehicle's glove compartment"; "no expiration date and is non-transferable. It becomes invalid upon the sale or transfer of the vehicle"; owner "is responsible for having the sun screening material removed before selling/transferring the vehicle"; company vehicles need "written permission on the company's letterhead". Once a physician's statement is on file it is "permanent" and not needed again for additional vehicles. (HSMV 83391 is not a public download — 404.)
- Practical note for an installer: the exemption attaches to a specific VIN; ask to see the 83391 certificate (or a copy) before installing beyond the limits — the installer misdemeanor in 316.2956(3) has no express medical-exemption carve-out other than the customer's certificate under 316.29545(1).

**Recommended label (meets all three statutory content elements; the rest is optional but useful):**
Material spec to order: white destructible ("eggshell"/tamper-evident) vinyl, permanent acrylic adhesive, no laminate, roughly 2" x 3" (fits the B-pillar/door-jamb area next to the OEM tire/VIN labels). Apply to a clean, dry painted jamb; most vendors say the adhesive needs 24–72 hours to fully bond.

```
FLORIDA WINDOW SUNSCREENING COMPLIANCE LABEL
This sunscreening material complies with
Sections 316.2951–316.2954, Florida Statutes.
Film trade name: ______________________
Installer: [YOUR LEGAL BUSINESS NAME]
Coral Springs, FL · (xxx) xxx-xxxx
Front side windows ≥28% VLT / ≤25% reflectance
Behind driver ≥15% VLT (≥6% multipurpose passenger vehicle) / ≤35% reflectance
Installed: ____/____/______   VLT installed: F ____%  R ____%
Self-destructive label — do not attempt to remove.
```
Statutory minimum is the first three items (compliance statement, trade name, business name). Pre-print your business name; either pre-print one variant per film line you carry (e.g., "XPEL PRIME XR PLUS") or leave a write-in line — the statute requires the label to "state" the trade name but does not say it must be printed. Keep the invoice with film name and VLT per window as your own record.

## 4. Enforcement reality

- **Installer/seller prosecutions under 316.2956(3):** none found. Ten-plus news searches ("tint shop owner arrested/charged/cited Florida", "tint enforcement operation/sting", "316.2956 installer charged") returned only law-firm explainers and driver-stop stories. CourtListener returns **zero** opinions containing "316.2955" other than Classy Cycles, Inc. v. Bay County, 201 So. 3d 779 (Fla. 1st DCA 2016), which merely lists the tint statutes among traffic laws in a preemption case; and the "316.2956" hits are all driver traffic-stop/suppression cases: State v. Moore, 791 So. 2d 1246 (Fla. 1st DCA 2001); Dorelus v. State, 747 So. 2d 368 (Fla. 1999); Davis v. State, 788 So. 2d 308 (Fla. 5th DCA 2001); Richardson v. State, 971 So. 2d 295 (Fla. 4th DCA 2008); United States v. Collins, No. 23-10322 (11th Cir. Aug. 27, 2024) (unpublished). Also State v. Wimberly, 988 So. 2d 116 (Fla. 5th DCA 2008) (per pawuklaw.com summary: officer reasonably believed tint too dark, tint turned out legal, evidence not suppressed). Full opinion texts could not be retrieved (CourtListener pages are JS-gated; API returns 401), but the retrieved 11th Cir. opinion United States v. Weaver, No. 04-15986 (Aug. 10, 2005) (https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/unpub/files/200415986.pdf) quotes 316.2953 and holds probable cause existed where the deputy "could not see the dashboard lights or driver's silhouette through the windows at close range," relying on Moore ("finding probable cause existed that the window tint statute was violated because officer was unable to 'make out the [vehicle's] occupant'"). Practical takeaway: Florida courts treat dark tint as a lawful primary basis for a stop; the meter reading (with ±3 tolerance) is what the citation rests on.
- **Do officers check the door-jamb label?** No official FHP procedure mentions it (Policy 17.27 covers only the meter). No news or court report of a stop turning on the label was found. The only industry chatter located: TintDude threads "comply stickers" (topic 49716), "Door stickers" (77112) and "Florida tint law help" (62106), and a Facebook "Window Tint" group post titled "Florida window tint compliance stickers requirements?" — all blocked from direct reading; the search-engine snippet for the TintDude Florida thread paraphrased installers as saying they had "yet to run into anyone doing it" (i.e., applying the labels). Treat that as anecdotal and unverified. A secondary site (davidwoodwindowtinting.com) asserts that a legitimate installer sticker "is the easiest way to defuse a Florida traffic stop" — marketing, not evidence.
- **Driver-side enforcement is active:** e.g., AOL/Ledger story "Orlando Man's Dark Window Tint Leads To Felony Charges, Scuffle With Polk Deputies" (https://www.aol.com/articles/orlando-man-dark-window-tint-140721977.html) and Yahoo "They were pulled over for tinted windows in Florida…" (https://news.yahoo.com/pulled-over-tinted-windows-florida-183723332.html). These are driver stops, not installer cases.
- **Legislation:** no 2025/2026 bill amending 316.2955/316.2956 surfaced; the labeling section's history ends with ch. 94-306 (1994).
